On 6 November 2026, GRACE held its launch workshop online. The call for participants drew 42 registrations from 13 countries across four continents, and 28 people took part on the day.

Participants came from decision-making research, behavioural economics, clinical psychology, neuroscience and cognitive science, organisational behaviour and international business, dance, coaching and computer vision, as well as from industry. Twenty-two stayed for the full afternoon of small-group discussions and plenary.

## Three questions, three provocations

The workshop followed an unconference format. Before the event, participants shared the topics they most wanted to discuss on a shared board, and these set the agenda for small groups working on three questions:

1. What changes as people become more able to engage productively with uncertainty?
2. What would count as evidence of that change?
3. How might that change show in people's observable behaviour?

Three provocations opened the afternoon:

- **Making friends with uncertainty**, Mark Freeston, Newcastle University
- **Turning ambiguity into risk, at a price**, Konstantinos Georgalos, Lancaster University
- **Interacting with uncertainty**, Gaëlle Vallée-Tourangeau, Kingston University

The recordings are now available on the [workshop page](/event/grace-workshop-1/#recordings).

## What comes next

The discussions are feeding into an open workshop report setting out a shared working definition. Thirty-one participants contributed to the discussions, and contributors will be invited to co-author the report, with each contribution credited using [CRediT roles](https://credit.niso.org/). Our second workshop, with creative practitioners and coaches, took place on 13 November. A studio pilot follows in January 2027, then a methodological workshop led by Professor Dimitrios Makris in February, and the project closes with an in-person synthesis event in March 2027.
